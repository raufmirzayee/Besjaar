# Dutch Slang: 35 Words and Phrases You'll Actually Hear in the Netherlands

Your Dutch textbook taught you *hallo*, *dank je wel* and *hoe gaat het?* Then you sat on a train in Amsterdam or Rotterdam and heard *"Wollah mattie, die patta zijn echt vet"*, and none of it made sense.

This guide to **Dutch slang** covers the words locals really use. It starts with everyday informal Dutch your colleagues use and moves on to the **street slang (*straattaal*)** that young people across the Netherlands have adopted. Each word comes with its meaning, an example sentence and, where we know it, where it came from.

## Why Dutch slang is so interesting

Dutch slang mixes a lot of languages. Much of today's street language comes from:

- **Sranan Tongo**, the creole language of Suriname, a former Dutch colony
- **Papiamento**, spoken on Aruba, Curaçao and Bonaire
- **Moroccan Arabic** and **Berber (Tamazight)**
- **Turkish**
- **English**, mostly through hip-hop, gaming and social media

This mix is called ***straattaal*** (literally "street language"). It started in multicultural neighbourhoods in cities like Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague. Today you'll hear it in schoolyards everywhere, in Dutch rap, and all over TikTok.

## Everyday informal Dutch (safe to use anywhere)

You can use these with colleagues, friends and your Dutch in-laws without raising any eyebrows.

| Slang | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Vet** | Cool, awesome (literally "fat") | *Dat concert was echt vet!* (That concert was really cool!) |
| **Gaaf** | Great, cool | *Gave schoenen!* (Cool shoes!) |
| **Lekker** | Nice, good, pleasant (not only for food) | *Lekker weertje vandaag.* (Nice weather today.) |
| **Gezellig** | Cosy, fun, sociable. There's no exact English word for it | *Het was heel gezellig gisteren.* (Last night was really fun.) |
| **Toppie** | Great, perfect | *Morgen om acht? Toppie!* (Tomorrow at eight? Perfect!) |
| **Chill** | Relaxed, nice | *Chill dat je er bent.* (Nice that you're here.) |
| **Kapot** | Extremely (literally "broken") | *Ik ben kapot moe.* (I'm dead tired.) |
| **Brak** | Hungover, wrecked | *Ik ben zo brak van gisteren.* (I'm so hungover from last night.) |
| **Doei** | Bye | *Doei, tot morgen!* (Bye, see you tomorrow!) |
| **Kakker** | Posh, preppy person | *Hij doet zo kakkerig.* (He acts so posh.) |
| **Patser** | Flashy show-off with money | *Kijk die patser in z'n Lamborghini.* (Look at that show-off in his Lamborghini.) |
| **Balen** | To be bummed out | *Ik baal ervan dat het regent.* (I'm bummed that it's raining.) |
| **Sowieso** | Definitely, anyway | *Ik kom sowieso.* (I'm definitely coming.) |

💡 **Tip:** if you learn only one word from this list, make it ***gezellig***. The Dutch use it constantly, and they're delighted when a foreigner uses it correctly.

## Dutch street slang (*straattaal*)

These words come from the streets, from rap and from the group chat. Use them with friends your age. Your manager probably won't appreciate them.

### Greetings and people

| Slang | Meaning | Origin | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fakka / Fawaka** | What's up? / Hey | Sranan Tongo *fa waka* ("how's it going") | *Fakka mattie!* (What's up, bro!) |
| **Ewa** | Hey / Well | Moroccan Arabic *iwa* | *Ewa, alles goed?* (Hey, all good?) |
| **Mattie** | Friend, mate | Sranan Tongo *mati* | *Hij is m'n mattie.* (He's my mate.) |
| **Sahbi** | Friend, buddy | Arabic *sahbi* | *Wacht even, sahbi.* (Wait a sec, buddy.) |
| **Bro / Broer** | Brother, friend | English / Dutch | *Broer, waar ben je?* (Bro, where are you?) |
| **Sma** | Girl, woman | Sranan Tongo | *Wie is die sma?* (Who's that girl?) |
| **Dombo** | Idiot | Street slang | *Doe niet zo dombo.* (Don't be an idiot.) |

### Money, stuff and places

| Slang | Meaning | Origin | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Doekoe** | Money | Sranan Tongo | *Ik heb geen doekoe.* (I don't have any money.) |
| **Skeer** | Broke | Street slang | *Ik ben skeer tot vrijdag.* (I'm broke until Friday.) |
| **Osso** | House, home | Sranan Tongo *oso* | *Kom naar m'n osso.* (Come to my place.) |
| **Waggie** | Car | Sranan Tongo *wagi* | *Nieuwe waggie?* (New car?) |
| **Patta** | Sneakers, shoes | Sranan Tongo | *Fresh patta, bro.* (Fresh sneakers, bro.) |
| **Fissa** | Party | Papiamento / Spanish *fiesta* | *Kom je naar de fissa?* (Are you coming to the party?) |
| **Hosselen** | To hustle, make money on the side | English *hustle* | *Hij is altijd aan het hosselen.* (He's always hustling.) |

### Feelings, reactions and intensifiers

| Slang | Meaning | Origin | Example |

| **Faya** | Fire, amazing | Sranan Tongo *faya* | *Dat nummer is faya.* (That track is fire.) |
| **Tantoe** | Very, a lot | Street slang | *Tantoe lekker eten.* (Really good food.) |

| **Tagga** | To go, leave | Street slang | *Ik ga tagga.* (I'm heading off.) |

## English slang in Dutch

Dutch speakers, especially young ones, borrow a lot of English. Don't be surprised to hear sentences like:

- *"Dat is zo **cringe**."* (That's so cringe.)
- *"Hij is echt **random**."* (He's really random.)
- *"Ik ben **done** met school."* (I'm done with school.)
- *"**Same**, bro."*
- *"**Nice**, man!"*

These loanwords follow Dutch grammar, so you'll also hear Dutch verb forms like *gechilld* (chilled), *gecanceld* (cancelled) and *geliket* (liked).

---

## Three tips for using Dutch slang without sounding weird

1. **Know your audience.** *Vet*, *lekker* and *gezellig* work anywhere. *Wollah* and *doekoe* in a job interview will not land well.
2. **Listen first.** Slang varies by city, by age and even by neighbourhood. A word that's current in Amsterdam-West may sound dated in Groningen.
3. **Watch out for swearing.** Dutch is famous for swearing with diseases. These words are **very offensive**, much stronger than they sound to an English ear. Learn to recognise them, but don't use them.

## Frequently asked questions about Dutch slang

### What is *straattaal*?
*Straattaal* is Dutch street slang, an informal youth language that mixes Dutch with words from Sranan Tongo, Papiamento, Arabic, Berber, Turkish and English. It's widely used by young people in the Netherlands, both online and offline.

### What does *mattie* mean in Dutch?
*Mattie* means friend or mate. It comes from the Surinamese word *mati*, which also means friend.

### What does *wollah* mean?
*Wollah* means "I swear" and is used to stress that something is true. It comes from the Arabic *wallah* ("by God").

### What is the most typically Dutch word?
Most Dutch people would say ***gezellig***: a warm, cosy, sociable feeling. It isn't really slang, but you won't understand Dutch social life without it.

### Is it rude to use Dutch slang as a foreigner?
Not at all. Most Dutch people find it charming when learners use informal words like *vet* or *gezellig*. Just keep street slang for casual settings with friends.
